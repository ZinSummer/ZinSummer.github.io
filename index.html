About

My interest in organizations began in psychiatric wards. During clinical internships I met working adults whose anxiety and depression were tied to their jobs: relentless demands, tasks that did not seem to belong to their roles, and workplaces where no one noticed their struggle. I could help them cope, but I could not change the conditions they returned to. That led me from clinical psychology toward organizational research, where the goal is to prevent suffering rather than only ease it.

I am now an MSc student in Applied Psychology (Health and Clinical Psychology) at Peking University. My central question is: when work becomes less certain, what lets employees keep a sense of agency and certainty, and how can organizations create the conditions for it? I study this through ownership, status, and signaling, with a current focus on how AI changes the way expertise and skills are recognized at work.

My training combines psychology and law, and I work with multilevel, longitudinal, diary, and machine-learning methods. I collaborate with groups at Johns Hopkins University, City University of Hong Kong, The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, and Peking University. I also write an academic blog that summarizes organizational behavior and psychology research (1,600+ subscribers).

Research interests: Future of Work, Inclusive Workplace, Social Perception, Interpersonal Relationship
